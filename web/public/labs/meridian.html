Overview

Three controls. Nothing you have to learn twice.

01

The dial reads your intent, not your patience.

A 4,096-step optical encoder under 32 machined detents. Scrub a timeline frame by frame, walk a diff one hunk at a time, or spin it fast and let the acceleration curve carry you a thousand lines.

32 detents 4,096 steps/rev Push to commit
02

The display answers the question you keep alt-tabbing to ask.

A 128×32 monochrome panel with a documented socket. Pipe anything into it in nine lines of Python — build status, failing test count, the branch name you had already forgotten.

128×32 px 60 fps Local socket API
03

Every key has its own trigger depth.

Hall-effect switches read position continuously, so actuation is a number you choose per key: 0.2 mm for the ones you hammer all day, 3.6 mm for the one that force-pushes to main.

0.2–3.6 mm 1,000 Hz poll Hot-swap

Craft

The parts you never see are the ones that decide how it feels.

Machined, not moulded.

Each chassis starts as a 1.9 kg block of 6061-T6 and leaves the mill at 842 g. The tool marks on the underside are left in on purpose — bead-blasting them away would cost a day and hide the work.

Anodised three ways.

Type II anodising in Graphite, natural Silver and Midnight. The colour is grown into the oxide layer rather than sprayed on top, so the edge you rest your wrist against will not wear through to bare metal.

Serviceable to the last screw.

Six Torx T6 fasteners, no adhesive, no clips to snap. Switches, encoder, display and board are each replaceable on their own, and the schematics ship with the unit.

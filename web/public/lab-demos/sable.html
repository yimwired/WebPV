There is no radio in it.

No Wi-Fi chip, no Bluetooth, no cellular. Files leave over the USB-C port when you decide they should. We looked hard at putting a radio in and turning it off by default, and could not find a version of that which stays off after eighteen months of firmware updates.

The antenna cutout is still in the chassis CAD. We left it there so the second revision can be a different decision without new tooling.

The screen is slow on purpose.

The panel refreshes in 780 ms and there is no way to make it faster, so we spent the effort on the thing that actually matters while typing: keystroke-to-ink latency, which lands at 41 ms. Fast enough that the letters keep up with you. Slow enough that scrolling feels like a chore, which is the point.

Every 90 lines the panel does a full refresh and inverts for a fifth of a second. We tried hiding it. Hiding it made the ghosting worse.

Plain text, on a card you can hold.

Everything you write lands on a removable SD card as dated .txt files. No account, no sync, no export button. If Sable the company disappears next year, the words are still yours and still readable on anything made since 1993.

The card slot is under the folio hinge, not behind a door. Doors break.

A note on the second prototype

The first version had a touchscreen. It tested well and we killed it anyway, because every person we handed it to spent the first ten seconds swiping around looking for something to open.

The second version has a keyboard, one physical button, and a screen that only ever shows the file you are in. Handed to the same people, the first thing almost all of them did was start typing. That was the whole finding, and it cost us seven months.

We are a team of four in Rotterdam. The first run is 900 units because that is what we could pay for.
